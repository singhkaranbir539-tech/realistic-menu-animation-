ENVIRONMENT ACTUALLY AVAILABLE
  * Cloud container, 4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM, NO GPU.
  * Unreal Engine 5: NOT available (no GPU, no UE install possible). Fallback, as the spec allows:
    Blender 5.3 (installed from PyPI as the `bpy` module) with the Cycles path tracer on CPU.
  * FFmpeg 6 (system), Python 3.13 + numpy/scipy/scikit-image/nibabel/PIL.
  * AI video generation was considered (OpenArt connector) but the account only has 33 free credits,
    not enough for any video generation, so nothing generative was used for picture.

RENDER SETTINGS (all scenes)
  * Blender Cycles (CPU), path tracing, OpenImageDenoise (albedo+normal), adaptive sampling,
    22-28 samples/pixel (scene dependent), fast-GI approximation for bounce light, AgX view transform,
    camera motion blur, physical depth of field, Burley subsurface scattering on tissue/skin.
  * Sample counts actually used: scene 2 (brain) 28 spp at every frame (30 fps). All other shots
    were re-rendered in a time-saving mode after the client asked to "finish it asap": at most
    16 spp (adaptive threshold 0.06) and only every 2nd frame (15 fps). The in-between frames
    were synthesised with FFmpeg motion-compensated interpolation (minterpolate mci/aobmc/bidir)
    to rebuild 30 fps. LIMITATION: interpolated frames can show slight warping on fast or
    overlapping motion (particles, thin neurites), and lower sample counts mean somewhat more
    denoiser smoothing. At the end of each shot the final 1-2 frames repeat (no neighbour to
    interpolate from); these frames mostly sit inside the cross-transitions.
  * Internal render resolution 720x1280 (9:16), 16-bit PNG sequences.
    LIMITATION: native 2160x3840 Cycles rendering of 958 frames was not feasible on 4 CPU cores
    (measured 1-4 min per frame at 720p for the heavier shots). Frames are upscaled 3x to
    2160x3840 with Lanczos + light unsharp mask in compositing, then encoded. The file is true
    2160x3840, but the picture detail corresponds to a 720p render.
  * Lumen/Nanite (UE5-specific) do not exist in Blender; Cycles path tracing provides physically
    based global illumination instead. Volumetric fog was replaced by distance-based haze inside the
    shaders (and 2D light shafts in compositing) to keep render times feasible.

ASSETS (all original, open-licensed, or procedurally generated - no stock footage)
  * Human body, skeleton and organs: TotalSegmentator dataset v2.0.1, subject s1380 (whole-body CT
    + 117 segmentation masks), CC BY 4.0, Wasserthal et al., "TotalSegmentator: Robust Segmentation
    of 104 Anatomic Structures in CT Images", Radiology: AI 2023. Obtained via the pyvista data
    repository. Meshes made by marching cubes (scripts/generate_assets.py).
  * Folded cortex: FreeSurfer fsaverage5 pial surfaces + sulcal depth (distributed with nilearn),
    fitted into the subject's skull.
  * Neurons, astrocytes, perivascular space, red/white blood cells, platelets, muscle fibres,
    capillaries, hormone molecules, bedroom: procedurally generated in Python (scene scripts).
    Red blood cells use the Evans & Fung (1972) biconcave profile.
  * Font: Montserrat (SIL Open Font License), from the @expo-google-fonts/montserrat npm package.

DEVIATIONS FROM THE SPEC, AND WHY
  1. Face. The TotalSegmentator CT is DEFACED for patient privacy (the face is a smooth blob).
     A synthetic face would risk exactly the "artificial AI-looking face" the brief forbids, so the
     sleeper lies on their side facing away from camera; the camera approaches the head/shoulder and
     the comp "passes through the skin" into the anatomical view. In the glass-body shots the face
     region of the shell is smoothed to a neutral form.
  2. Arms / legs. CT patients are scanned with arms above the head and the scan ends at the upper
     thighs. The arms were digitally removed from the skin shell; the duvet in scene 1 is shaped
     over procedurally added legs; in the glass-body shots the anatomy fades out above the cut.
     Scene 5 therefore shows a head-to-pelvis anatomical figure, not a full standing body.
  3. Hair is Blender particle hair (Principled Hair BSDF); it is kept in soft focus/low key.
  4. Narration voice is a neural TTS voice, not a human narrator (see AUDIO).
  5. Resolution: see RENDER SETTINGS (720p render, upscaled to 4K).
  6. Frame rate of rendering: most shots rendered at 15 fps and interpolated to 30 fps (see above).
  7. Scene 4 re-edit: QC found jump cuts where the camera passed a white blood cell and dived into
     the muscle fibres faster than 15 fps interpolation could bridge. The edit now uses the vessel
     flight (rendered frames 1-100), dissolves through the vessel wall into the muscle-fibre shot
     (frames 126-148), slowed ~0.21x by motion-compensated interpolation (scripts/edit_scene04.py).
     The final close-up dive into a single fibre was dropped.
  8. Video bitrate capped at 22 Mbps (crf 15 otherwise) so the 4K file stays under 100 MB.

AUDIO
  * Narration: Kokoro-82M neural TTS (Apache-2.0), run locally; blended style vector
    (65% "am_michael" + 35% "am_onyx") for a deeper American male documentary read; per-line speed
    fitted to each scene slot (~150 wpm overall - the 70-word script cannot fit 30 s at 145 wpm with
    pauses). Processing: HPF 75 Hz, warm low-shelf, presence boost, de-esser, 3:1 compression,
    subtle synthetic room reverb.
  * Word timing for subtitles and an intelligibility check: sherpa-onnx Zipformer English ASR
    (Apache-2.0) run on every narration line and again on the final mix (see qc_report.json).
  * Music: original score synthesised in numpy (scripts/make_audio.py): detuned-saw pads through a
    moving filter (Dm(add9) - Bbmaj7 - Gm9 - F(add9) - Dm), additive "strings", sub pulses,
    plucked build, FM bells, cinematic toms and a final hit; structure follows the brief.
  * Sound effects: all synthesised (heartbeats, whooshes, impacts, electrical crackle, neural
    blips, underwater/liquid ambience, bubbles, riser), timed to picture.
  * Mix: music ~18 dB below narration (measured RMS while speaking) plus 4 dB sidechain ducking,
    SFX ~16.5 dB below narration, integrated loudness -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1.2 dBTP, 48 kHz stereo.

SCIENTIFIC CONTENT (claims kept conservative)
  * Sleep stages/memory: NREM/REM cycling and sleep-dependent memory consolidation are well supported.
  * Glymphatic clearance: CSF flows along perivascular spaces bounded by astrocyte end-feet and
    exchanges with interstitial fluid; clearance of metabolites such as amyloid-beta is enhanced
    during sleep (Xie et al., Science 2013). The visualisation shows CSF particles in the
    perivascular space and waste aggregates drifting into that stream - a schematic, not a literal
    "cleaning machine". (The magnitude of sleep-dependent clearance in humans is still researched.)
  * Repair: heart rate and breathing slow in NREM sleep; growth-hormone release peaks in slow-wave
    sleep and supports tissue maintenance - shown as hormone molecules leaving capillaries and binding
    at muscle-fibre surfaces (schematic).
  * Scales are approximately consistent (RBC ~7.8 um diameter, arteriole ~50 um, muscle fibre ~50 um).
